1About Headway Cardiff and South East Wales

Who they are, and what you're walking into

Headway Cardiff and South East Wales is an independent charity — separate from Headway UK, though it shares the name and works in partnership with it — supporting people affected by acquired brain injury (ABI) to rebuild their lives and regain independence. It was founded in 1985 as a support group by ABI patients, families and therapists, and today supports around 500–600 people a year across Cardiff, the Vale of Glamorgan, Bridgend, RCT, Merthyr, Newport, Blaenau Gwent, Caerphilly and Monmouthshire.

Services span an Information & Outreach team (first point of contact), welfare benefits support, counselling, a monthly carers' group, weekly walking/wheeling groups, and an Independence & Wellbeing Centre (based at University Hospital Llandough, with community sessions elsewhere) — around 100 of the 500–600 people supported each year attend the centre itself.

It's a small organisation — 15 staff, most part-time, backed by around 60 volunteers, including a core of psychology placement students who rotate through on nine-month blocks. Income is just over £540k a year, roughly 70% from statutory contracts with local authorities and health boards (day-centre places, essentially), and the rest from donations, corporate sponsorship and its own fundraising. Reserves are healthy — well above the charity's own target — so this is a stable, sustainably-run organisation, not one in crisis. It picked up a national Volunteer of the Year award in 2025 and was an Innovation Award finalist in 2023.

Mission: "To promote understanding of all aspects of acquired brain injury and to provide information, support, and services to people with acquired brain injury, their families and carers." Headway is built around a simple ambition — that people with ABI and their families aren't isolated or disadvantaged, that they get real support to adapt to life after injury, and that their independence, skills and quality of life are maximised.

The role itself: this is a genuinely senior post — reporting directly to Rebecca as CEO, and a key priority hire for Headway to increase the charity's management capacity. You'd manage two administrators (finance and people/HR), hold Data Protection Officer responsibility, act as designated fire warden and office first aider, and formally deputise for the CEO when required — including attending Trustee meetings. It's based at University Hospital Llandough, three days a week in the office with the rest home-based, 37 hours a week, and occasionally evenings or weekends.

Why this matters for you: this is a lean, relationship-driven charity where the back office has historically been held together by very few people. That's the gap you'd be stepping into — and it's a very similar shape to where PROPS was a few years into your own time there, which is worth having in your back pocket if it comes up.

3Why This Role — Preparing Your Answers

Use your own real story. It's a strong one.

Why you're ready to move on

You've already said it plainly, and it's the truest answer you've got: it's time to go. You joined PROPS when it was roughly Headway's size now — eight staff — and you built up real depth across the whole back office because that's what a small charity needs. PROPS has since grown to 75 staff, and you're still the one holding month-end together with just 15 hours a week of assistant support. You've asked for help and it's taken a long time to get even that.

"It's still me doing all that stuff... I'm doing everything."

Be matter-of-fact about this rather than resentful — Rebecca will recognise the shape of it immediately, because it's exactly the gap she's trying to close at Headway before it happens to her.

Why Headway specifically

You put it well yourself: Headway sounds like what PROPS was a few years ago. That's a genuine, specific reason to want this — you're not just leaving PROPS, you're choosing to go somewhere the size and shape suits the way you like to work, where your breadth of experience is exactly what's needed, and where you'd be building the function properly from an earlier stage rather than firefighting inside one that's outgrown its support.

"It's coming purely within the charity sector. I'm not interested in making money."

That line is worth having ready in some form. Rebecca isn't going to be won over by ambition or salary talk — she'll respond to genuine commitment to the sector and to Headway's mission specifically. If you know a little about what Headway actually does day to day (see Section 1), work it into your answer naturally rather than reciting facts.

Headway's own values — worth knowing before you go in

Headway sets out its expected values under four headings: being attentive, empathetic, respectful and empowering towards the people they support; being sincere, trustworthy and just — reliable and honest in what you say and do; being clear and professional in how you communicate and hold yourself accountable; and being competent, reliable, safe, open and accountable — including being willing to recognise mistakes, raise concerns, and keep learning. These aren't abstract — they're the actual framework Headway measures its people against, so if you're asked what values mean to you or to give an example of living them out, it's worth quietly anchoring your real examples (the fraud disclosure, the expenses policy, the QuickBooks self-teaching) to language like this rather than generic "I care about people" answers.

What to avoid

Try not to let the PROPS story tip into complaint. You lived through a genuinely hard period there — a financial crisis, working 10–11 hour days holding things together, being told you'd be looked after and then not — and it's fair to be honest about that. But frame it as evidence of what you're capable of under pressure, not as a grievance. Rebecca will draw her own conclusions about PROPS; you don't need to editorialise.

5Areas to Prepare

Be honest, not defensive — Rebecca values straight answers over polish

Budget-building & audit exposure

Your experience has been more operational than strategic

Most of your depth is in the day-to-day running of the finance function — payroll, month end, corrections, compliance — rather than building a budget from scratch or leading an audit file end to end. If asked about this, don't overclaim. Be honest that your exposure has mostly come from being the one who lives inside the numbers day to day, and frame that as a strong foundation to build formal budgeting and audit-lead experience on top of, rather than a gap you're covering for.

Systems & vendor management

Lean on the QuickBooks story, and be upfront about the rest

If asked about experience with accounting software, contracts or negotiating with suppliers/vendors, the QuickBooks migration is your strongest card — use it. If pushed further on vendor negotiation specifically and you don't have much to point to, say so plainly and pivot to how you approach learning something new (see Section 4) — that's a more convincing answer than stretching a thin example.

Health & safety for a physical building

Think this through beforehand — it's a real, live part of the role

Headway runs a day centre and community sessions from physical premises, so health & safety here isn't a policy exercise — it's noticing risks day to day, actioning them, and knowing the fire evacuation procedure cold. If your current H&S exposure is lighter or more remote-based, it's worth spending some time before the interview thinking practically: what would you check first walking into a new building, how would you familiarise yourself with an evacuation procedure, what would "good" look like. You don't need to be an expert — you need to show you'd take it seriously and get up to speed fast.

There will be a written task

As part of this stage, expect a short timed written exercise — roughly an hour — covering practical back-office scenarios similar to what you'd meet in the role day to day. Based on what's been shared with you, expect it to touch on a payroll or salary-cost style calculation, and some thinking around new-starter administration and health & safety risk areas for a building. You won't see the specifics beforehand, and that's deliberate — it's designed to test how you think on your feet and work through a practical problem methodically, not what you've memorised or rehearsed. Read each part carefully, show your workings clearly, note any assumptions you make, and don't be afraid to flag if something's ambiguous rather than guessing silently.

6What the Conversation Will Likely Explore

General themes, not the actual questions — come with real examples ready for each

Without giving away the specifics, expect the interview to explore territory like:

  • How your skills and experience map onto what the role actually needs day to day
  • Your familiarity with accounting/finance software and general office administration practices
  • Your understanding of good HR practice and employment principles
  • What Headway's values mean to you personally (see Section 3 for the actual framework), and how you'd bring them to life practically — not just in words
  • A real example of a mistake you've made and how you handled putting it right
  • A difficult people or HR-related situation you've navigated, and how you approached it
  • What "going beyond your role" looks like for you at a small charity

You already have strong, real material for every one of these — see Section 4. The best preparation isn't guessing the exact questions; it's having two or three specific, concrete stories ready that you can adapt to whatever's actually asked.

7Key Things to Remember

  • Genuine enthusiasm for Headway's mission, not just "a charity role"
