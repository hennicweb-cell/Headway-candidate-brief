Please read before reviewing scores

The assessment, scores and commentary in this report represent my interpretation of Holly's performance across CV review, first interview and structured competency interview. It is not a definitive assessment of her capability or potential.

Scores are based on specific evidence gathered through the process — her written application, her responses to the questions asked at first interview, and her responses to the structured competency questions. This report is intended as a guide to inform your own assessment at interview — not as a final verdict. The scores and commentary should prompt lines of enquiry, not close them down. The final assessment and hiring decision rests entirely with you and Lisa.

How scores are calculated

Holly is assessed across the ten dimensions of the agreed scoring framework, each weighted according to the priorities you set out. A score out of 10 is given per dimension, multiplied by the weighting to produce a weighted score. The total is out of 100. Where a dimension was tested at both first and competency interview, the final score is a straight average of the two. Her Data Protection & Compliance and Practical Fit & Logistics dimensions were not re-tested at competency stage, so the first-interview scores stand alone per the agreed methodology.

Assessment Summary

Holly's profile is defined by genuinely resilient financial control and integrity evidence, forged under difficult real circumstances. She inherited her current role in the middle of an undisclosed fraud investigation with no handover, and personally designed a real, working dual-control payment process in response — her finance officer completes bank reconciliations on paper, independently cross-checked by her, alongside role-based bank access limits and second-manager sign-off. Her account of making investment decisions and leading board meetings alone while deputising for her CEO is direct, specific and consistent across both interviews.

Her people-management evidence is the richest of any candidate assessed so far. She gave a genuine, sensitive account of rebuilding a colleague's confidence after he discovered the underlying fraud, and handled a difficult grievance investigation into her own CEO appropriately, escalating correctly rather than acting alone. Her best answer of either interview was on ownership and integrity: a roughly £30,000 tax payment went out three days late after a direct debit silently failed to set up, and she disclosed it to her CEO immediately and unprompted, took full responsibility without deflecting, and corrected it the same day.

IT and systems remain her comparatively weakest area — she was honest that she has never built a formal system or reporting tool beyond Excel, and the specific IT-contract negotiation question at competency stage wasn't something she'd done. A small number of competency answers (people management, autonomy) reused or left in-progress evidence rather than fresh, closed examples — though this looks more like honest, unpolished answering under pressure than a genuine gap; she was candid, unprompted, that she felt she'd rambled and hadn't delivered her best in the competency interview.
