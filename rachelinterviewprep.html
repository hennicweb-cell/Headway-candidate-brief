1About Headway Cardiff and South East Wales

Who they are, and what you're walking into

Headway Cardiff and South East Wales is an independent charity — separate from Headway UK, though it shares the name and works in partnership with it — supporting people affected by acquired brain injury (ABI) to rebuild their lives and regain independence. It was founded in 1985 as a support group by ABI patients, families and therapists, and today supports around 500–600 people a year across Cardiff, the Vale of Glamorgan, Bridgend, RCT, Merthyr, Newport, Blaenau Gwent, Caerphilly and Monmouthshire.

Services span an Information & Outreach team (first point of contact), welfare benefits support, counselling, a monthly carers' group, weekly walking/wheeling groups, and an Independence & Wellbeing Centre (based at University Hospital Llandough, with community sessions elsewhere) — around 100 of the 500–600 people supported each year attend the centre itself.

It's a small organisation — 15 staff, most part-time, backed by around 60 volunteers, including a core of psychology placement students who rotate through on nine-month blocks. Income is just over £540k a year, roughly 70% from statutory contracts with local authorities and health boards (day-centre places, essentially), and the rest from donations, corporate sponsorship and its own fundraising. Reserves are healthy — well above the charity's own target — so this is a stable, sustainably-run organisation, not one in crisis. It picked up a national Volunteer of the Year award in 2025 and was an Innovation Award finalist in 2023.

Mission: "To promote understanding of all aspects of acquired brain injury and to provide information, support, and services to people with acquired brain injury, their families and carers." Headway is built around a simple ambition — that people with ABI and their families aren't isolated or disadvantaged, that they get real support to adapt to life after injury, and that their independence, skills and quality of life are maximised.

The role itself: this is a genuinely senior post — reporting directly to Rebecca as CEO, and a key priority hire for Headway to increase the charity's management capacity. You'd manage two administrators (finance and people/HR), hold Data Protection Officer responsibility, act as designated fire warden and office first aider, and formally deputise for the CEO when required — including attending Trustee meetings. It's based at University Hospital Llandough, three days a week in the office with the rest home-based, 37 hours a week, and occasionally evenings or weekends.

Why this matters for you: at just over £540k income and 15 staff, Headway is a smaller organisation than Cardiff Foodbank, and this is a deliberately narrower, deeper role than the CEO breadth you carry now — finance, governance and organisational development, without the full weight of external representation, fundraising leadership and being the final decision-maker on everything. That's exactly the shift your own application describes wanting to make.

3Why This Role — Preparing Your Answers

Use your own real story. It's a genuinely compelling one.

Why you're moving away from CEO — and why it matters here

Your own application already puts this well, and it's worth having a confident, settled version of it ready: three years as Chief Executive of Cardiff Foodbank has given you real clarity about where your energy and strengths actually lie — finance, governance and organisational development — rather than the full breadth of CEO leadership. That's not a step down or a retreat; it's a deliberate move toward the parts of the job you're most energised by, in a role that lets you go deeper rather than wider. Say this plainly and with conviction — it will land far better than an apologetic or defensive framing.

"I am particularly energised by finance, governance, organisational development and enabling charities to operate effectively, compliantly and sustainably."

The CEO-to-subordinate question — expect this directly

This is the single most important thing to prepare for. Both Rebecca and Lisa will want a genuine, specific answer — not a reassurance — on whether you can take direction and act as a point of contact rather than the final decision-maker, after three years of being exactly that. Have a real example ready of a time you deferred to someone else's call even when you might have decided differently yourself (from your board relationships, your time at Trussell advising rather than deciding, or elsewhere) — a lived instance is far more convincing than a general assurance that you'd be fine with it.

Headway's own values — worth knowing before you go in

Headway sets out its expected values under four headings: being attentive, empathetic, respectful and empowering towards the people they support; being sincere, trustworthy and just — reliable and honest in what you say and do; being clear and professional in how you communicate and hold yourself accountable; and being competent, reliable, safe, open and accountable — including being willing to recognise mistakes, raise concerns, and keep learning. Your Beacon data breach story is a genuinely strong illustration of the last of these — surfacing a problem transparently rather than concealing it — worth anchoring explicitly to this language rather than leaving it as a general competence example.

What to avoid

Watch your instinct to speak from "CEO mode" — with the authority and ownership that comes naturally after three years as the final word. It's a genuine strength in the right places, but if it comes across as difficulty taking direction, it will work against you here. Also be ready to speak plainly and specifically about wanting this narrower, deeper role rather than letting it sound like a stopgap on the way back to another CEO position.

There will be a written task

As part of this stage, expect a short timed written exercise — roughly an hour — covering practical back-office scenarios similar to what you'd meet in the role day to day. Based on what's been shared with you, expect it to touch on a payroll or salary-cost style calculation, and some thinking around new-starter administration and health & safety risk areas for a building. You won't see the specifics beforehand, and that's deliberate — it's designed to test how you think on your feet and work through a practical problem methodically, not what you've memorised or rehearsed. Read each part carefully, show your workings clearly, note any assumptions you make, and don't be afraid to flag if something's ambiguous rather than guessing silently.

6What the Conversation Will Likely Explore

General themes, not the actual questions — come with real examples ready for each

Without giving away the specifics, expect the interview to explore territory like:

  • How your skills and experience map onto what the role actually needs day to day
  • Your familiarity with accounting/finance software and general office administration practices
  • Your understanding of good HR practice and employment principles
  • What Headway's values mean to you personally (see Section 3 for the actual framework), and how you'd bring them to life practically — not just in words
  • A real example of a mistake you've made and how you handled putting it right
  • A difficult people or HR-related situation you've navigated, and how you approached it
  • What "going beyond your role" looks like for you at a small charity

You already have strong, real material for every one of these — see Section 4. The best preparation isn't guessing the exact questions; it's having two or three specific, concrete stories ready that you can adapt to whatever's actually asked.
