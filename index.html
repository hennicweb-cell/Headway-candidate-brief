About Headway Cardiff and South East Wales

A charity that has stayed close to the people it serves.

Headway Cardiff and South East Wales began in 1985 as a support group at Rookwood specialist rehabilitation hospital, started by people with acquired brain injury (ABI), their families and their therapists. Forty-one years on, it's grown into a small, well-regarded local charity supporting 500–600 people a year across the Cardiff and Vale, Aneurin Bevan and Cwm Taf Morgannwg University Health Board areas — but it's stayed close to its roots. People with ABI still sit on the board.

The charity runs an Information & Outreach service, an Independence & Wellbeing day centre based at Llandough (plus satellite sessions in the community), a counselling service, monthly family and carer support, and weekly walking and wheeling groups. Funding comes mainly from spot-contracted centre places with local authorities and health boards, topped up by corporate sponsorship, donations and the charity's own fundraising — deliberately avoiding heavy reliance on time-limited grants.

"We develop incrementally, cautious never to jeopardise what we've already built, but ambitious to improve and grow to meet the needs of people with acquired brain injury as effectively as we can, as demand increases. We're small enough, and agile enough, that when something needs doing, or an opportunity arises, we can just get on and seize it." — Rebecca Pearce, CEO
The Role

Business Director / Manager

You'll run all the vital behind-the-scenes work that makes it possible for our services staff and volunteers to provide direct support to the people who need us — making sure the centre is a safe, pleasant environment for everyone who uses it, that staff have the resources and systems they need to get on with the important stuff, and that the finances are in place and properly accounted for. You won't do this alone: you'll become the third member of our management team, with the CEO supporting you and helping you grow into the role so she can step back from the day-to-day doing to focus more on overseeing, developing and building external relationships.

We don't expect you to arrive with all the experience already in place. What matters most is the ability to learn quickly, spot ways to improve and innovate, think critically and strategically, research independently, and make good decisions — including supporting service staff through challenging situations when the other managers aren't around.

💷

Finance & Budgets

Own the annual budget, monitor spend against it, second-authorise payments, and work with the external accountant and Treasurer on the annual accounts and trustees' report. Payroll runs through Xero — full training given if you haven't run it before.

📊

Grant & Funder Reporting

Prepare forecast budgets for funded projects, track spend against them, and report back to funders accurately and on time. If you've built a budget into a grant application and reported on it, this will feel immediately familiar.

💻

IT & Systems

Review the current IT support arrangement, negotiate a better deal, and keep systems and cyber security fit for purpose. Genuinely strong Excel skills matter here more than any particular software background.

🗂️

Office & People Administration

Line-manage the finance and people administrators — supervision, annual reviews, expenses, recruitment — and keep office administration, health & safety and data protection running smoothly.

🤝

Senior Cover

Become the third senior decision-maker on site, alongside the CEO and Director of Services, so there's always someone who can make the call when something happens.

🚀

Room to Shape It

The CEO currently carries all of this herself. There's real, unclaimed scope here to bring order to it, improve it, and make parts of it your own.

The Opportunity

Where this can lead.

This isn't a role with a fixed ceiling. The CEO is explicit that she's looking for someone with the ambition and judgement to grow into a much bigger remit over time — potentially her own.

A genuinely open runway

A real, stated path toward CEO — over roughly 7 to 10 years.

Headway is deliberately keeping this role open rather than narrowly defined, because the organisation wants someone who can grow with it — someone who starts by taking real ownership of finance and operations, earns trust quickly, and is given more as they prove themselves. Rebecca has been clear that she's looking for someone who could realistically develop into the CEO role over the next 7–10 years.

How far and how fast that goes is genuinely up to you. It's a blank canvas in the parts that aren't fixed — the more trust you earn, the wider the role becomes. What Rebecca wants isn't someone who does exactly what the job description says and nothing more; it's someone who comes in asking "how can I make this better," backs it up with a proper business case, and is given the room to run with it.
