Assessment Summary

Her profile is defined by two genuinely exceptional data points sitting alongside a deep, fully-verified financial competence. Her financial control and payroll evidence is the clearest, most consistent strength in the assessment — a colour-coded payroll tracking system, weekly check-ins, and two years of solo audit ownership, confirmed in detail under direct competency questioning. She volunteered, unprompted, that she checks her own work against the payroll company's return: "I'm not just a double checker, I'm a triple checker."

Her autonomy evidence is the standout of the whole assessment. Beyond stepping up as the most senior member of staff when PROPS lost both its CEO and Head of Operations, she disclosed at competency interview that she personally raised suspicions of fraud to the trustees — a high-stakes, courageous, correctly-judged decision that triggered a formal investigation, the CEO's departure, and a police case that remains ongoing. Her values evidence is concrete and repeated rather than one-off: she is one of only two staff who support trainees on PROPS' annual long-distance cycle ride (up at 5am to drive a minibus and lead the shorter route), and she organises an annual charity rugby match with her son.

The gaps that remain — health & safety, annual budget preparation, and IT/systems contract experience — are honestly disclosed and now genuinely tested rather than simply absent from evidence. On each, she reasoned through a sound general approach but was candid that she has no direct hands-on experience, which is consistent with someone who has operated in a smaller finance-only remit than the fuller office-management brief this role requires.

On practical fit, directly asked how many days she could commit to being on site, she gave a clear, considered answer: three days as an absolute regular maximum because of the commute, with flexibility for more during onboarding or short-term cover — matching the role's stated three-day minimum exactly.

Combined Score: 72.5 / 100

Worth resolving three small practical points she raised at competency stage before an offer stage: office parking availability, whether the three-day office requirement has flexibility once she's settled in, and her current annual leave entitlement (30 days, against the role's 22 rising to 29 after five years). None are deal-breakers on her side, but worth a clear answer.

Red flags

  • Health & safety remains the weakest dimension across both interviews — sound general knowledge, but speculative on fire evacuation specifically, with no hands-on experience.
  • No practical experience preparing an annual budget or negotiating an IT/systems contract — both confirmed as genuine gaps rather than absence of evidence.
  • No experience running payroll on an accounting/payroll platform directly — PROPS' payroll runs through an external provider.
  • Practical points raised at competency stage (parking, day-flexibility, annual leave) worth resolving before offer.

Green flags

  • Two independent, high-stakes autonomy examples on record — stepping up through a leadership gap, and personally raising fraud concerns to the trustees.
  • Financial control and payroll evidence is detailed, systematic and fully confirmed under direct questioning — the clearest strength in the profile.
  • Values evidence is concrete and repeated, not a one-off — sustained personal volunteering beyond her job description.
  • Consistently honest under pressure, including correcting the interviewer's own assumptions rather than embellishing.
  • Her on-site commitment (three days, clearly reasoned) matches the role's stated minimum exactly.
